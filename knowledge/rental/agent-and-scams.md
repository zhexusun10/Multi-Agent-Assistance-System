# Agent identity, commissions and rental scams

## Check more than a name
The curated police guidance warns that scammers can impersonate registered agents using real names and registration numbers. Use the CEA Public Register to check whether the contact’s phone number matches the actual registration, view the home in person and verify who you are dealing with. A business card, profile photo, registration number, listing link or WhatsApp chat alone does not establish the person’s identity.

## Requests for money before viewing
Police guidance says not to pay an agent for a viewing. Rent and rental deposits should go directly to the landlord through verifiable payment methods, not a supposed assistant or an unverified account. Project advice: pause payment if someone demands money before giving an address or claims an overseas landlord will courier keys. Verify independently through official contact details rather than acting under pressure. Retain adverts, phone numbers, chats and payment details for advice or reporting.

## No uniform fixed commission rate
CEA states that agent commissions have no fixed rate or prescribed amount. Agree the amount, conditions and GST beforehand and record them in the estate agency agreement; agreed commission is payable to the estate agency. A half-month commission in an advertisement is not a universal legal standard. Another party’s agent working on the transaction does not automatically mean the user owes that fee. Clarify who represents whom, the services and when liability arises.

## Contact-evidence limits in this project
A phone in historical listing data does not prove it is currently used by that agent or supports WhatsApp/Telegram. A pasted reply is communication supplied by the user. A model may interpret its terms without independently verifying the contact’s identity, landlord’s ownership or authenticity of the listing.

## A traceable enquiry
Project advice: prepare an editable draft identifying the home and specific questions, then independently check the recipient and send it yourself. Confirming “I sent it” is a manual record, not a delivery receipt. Retain reply text and time. If it requests payment, identity documents or an unfamiliar website, verify independently first and consult official Singapore Police or ScamShield resources where needed.
